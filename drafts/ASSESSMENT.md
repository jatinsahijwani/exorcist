## Human review (2026-11-11)

Model used: `anthropic/claude-opus-5-5` through NEAR AI Cloud (`https://cloud-api.near.ai/v1`), from the
live model list. All five drafts compiled in the first round; the repair loop was not needed.

**Is it independent?** Yes. The prompt contains only the DSL reference and the Rust file. No draft
calls or copies our hand-written handlers (`grep vaultHandle|tokenHandle|hookHandle drafts/*.lean`
finds them only in the comparison harness).

| Draft | Review against the Rust | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `VaultFixed` | Same structure as our hand-written fixed vault: credit in `after_notify` only on success, report `amount` unused on failure, debit-then-`ft_transfer`-then-restore in `withdraw`/`after_withdraw`. | faithful |
| `VaultGhost` | Credits in `ft_on_transfer` and returns the hook promise (`.promise 0`), with the cargo feature resolved correctly; it even marks the line `-- BUG (ghost_funds_bug)`. | faithful |
| `VaultNoRestore` | `after_withdraw` ignores a failed transfer, matching the `withdraw_no_restore_bug` build. | faithful |
| `Token` | Matches the NEP-141 reference: 1-yocto checks, transfer inside `ft_transfer_call`, `resolve` computing `min(parsed or amount, amount)` and refunding `min(receiver balance, unused)`. Like our model, it leaves out the registration methods and the burn-on-missing-sender path; neither has a constructor in the DSL. | faithful (same abstraction as ours) |
| `Hook` | The hook's `mode` lives in the hook's own storage, which `St` cannot hold. The draft says so in a comment and models the mode as a fixed parameter (`handleWith .ok / .none / .panic`). `.ok` equals our `hookHandle (.nat 0)`, `.none` equals `hookHandle .unit`, and `.panic` matches a forced failure. Not part of the automatic check, reviewed by hand. | faithful, with a correctly stated limitation |

**Where the drafts differ from our hand-written models.** Every vault method in the drafts panics when
a deposit is attached ("doesn't accept deposit"). That is real near-sdk behaviour: non-`#[payable]`
methods reject attached deposits. Our hand-written vault does not model it. It changes no verdict here,
because no scenario attaches a deposit to a vault call, but on this point **the drafts are more faithful
than our model**. It is now on our list to add.

**Conclusion.** On these three small contracts, NEAR AI produced models that a reviewer would accept
after reading them. They match the hand-written models on every automatic check and in a line-by-line
read, and they caught one detail we had missed. This is evidence on three contracts we wrote ourselves,
with a model given a DSL reference. It is not a general claim. Drafts stay experimental and every line
still needs human review.
